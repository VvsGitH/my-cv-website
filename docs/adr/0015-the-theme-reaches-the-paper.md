# The theme reaches the paper, and stops at the cream

Dark mode used to be the smallest thing it could be: it repainted `body` and the Colophon's ink, nothing else. The Sheets stayed white paper in both themes, which was spec US17 in as many words. **The theme now reaches the paper.** The page behind the Sheets goes to a mid blue, the Sheet under it to a darker navy, and the inks travel from near-black to near-white with them.

Three things follow, and each is a decision rather than a detail.

**The page and the paper are two surfaces.** They were one token (`--color-main-bg`) and are now two, because the moment the paper is themed there is no colour left to describe the ground it lies on. `--color-page-bg` is that ground: an elegant grey `#eaebef` in light, `#2b3747` in dark. Note the inversion — in light the paper is *lighter* than the page, in dark it is *darker*. Both steps are ~1.13:1, which is enough to draw the Sheet's edge, so the Sheets still carry no border and no shadow. Reading Mode's reading surface takes `--color-main-bg` and therefore takes the theme from the Sheets it dismantled, rather than inventing a third answer.

**The cream does not participate.** The Aside's `#fef9e0` panel, the Drawer that carries the Aside's content into Reading Mode, and the Toolbar are the same surface in both themes — that was the brief, and it is also the constraint that shapes everything below. An ink that followed the theme would be white on cream at about **1.05:1**. So those three surfaces re-declare `--color-heading`, `--color-text` and `--color-muted` from the light ramp on their own root element, and every descendant inherits the light inks without knowing why. The Toolbar pins `--color-main-bg` alongside them, because its toast inverts against that token and its shadow mixes on `--color-heading`.

**The switch happens once, on `:root`.** `tokens.css` holds a raw `-light` / `-dark` pair per themed token and five semantic tokens that a ladder rebinds; no component reads a pair, and no component's colour declarations changed. The alternative — the three-step `light-dark()` ladder this project already used per property — would have had to be written out at roughly twenty call sites.

## Considered Options

- **Root-level `light-dark()` on the tokens themselves,** as `coding-standards.md` otherwise asks for. **Rejected, and the reason generalises:** a custom property stores its value unparsed, so the plain-value declaration before it is *not* discarded by a browser lacking `light-dark()` — the second declaration still wins and the colour fails at computed-value time, leaving the property unset. The fallback pattern only works where the browser parses the value at declaration time, which means per property, not per token. `reset.css` was the only place doing it that way, and it no longer needs to.
- **Inverting the direction: keep `:root` light and let the paper opt into dark ink.** Roughly symmetric on count — three surfaces need light ink, three need dark. Rejected because the Colophon and everything on the page backdrop would then need the opt-in too, and because "the theme is the default, the cream is the exception" is the truer sentence: the cream is one panel with a stated reason, and the page is everything else.
- **A shared global rule listing the three cream surfaces** (`.aside, .toolbar, .drawer { … }`). Rejected: it is exactly the cross-component global stylesheet the coding standards permit in only two named cases. Each surface declaring its own ink is three more lines and keeps each component's colours in the component.
- **Darkening the Aside too, and re-pitching the whole Sheet.** Not considered seriously — the cream panel is the CV's one piece of visual identity, and it is what the printed document looks like.

## Consequences

- **Print and the OG card can never go dark, and it costs no duplicated literal.** The entire ladder sits inside `@media screen`, so under `print` the base values — the light ones — are simply what remains. `screen and` was already load-bearing on this project's Reading Mode queries (ADR-0006, coding-standards); it is now load-bearing on the theme as well. `pdf.spec.ts` asserts it with `data-theme` still set to `dark`.
- **`og/[locale].astro` is pinned `data-theme="light"`,** having previously been pinned `dark` for a backdrop it no longer needs. The card paints its own ground from `--color-main-bg-dark`, and the Sheet on it must stay white paper. Explicit rather than absent, because the headless browser that screenshots the route brings a `prefers-color-scheme` of its own.
- **`--color-dark-bg` is gone,** renamed `--color-main-bg-dark` now that its job is the paper rather than the backdrop. ADR-0008's focus-ring measurements name the old token; its conclusion survives the rename intact — `--color-muted` still clears 3:1 on every surface this project rings, including **5.5:1** on the dark paper and **4.3:1** on the dark page, and inside the Aside and Toolbar it is the light value it always measured.
- **The Colophon lost its bespoke ladder.** `--colophon-ink` / `--colophon-ink-dark` and four rules collapsed to `color: var(--color-heading)`: it stands on the page background, and the background and its ink now move together on their own.
- **The two ink ramps are symmetric — fifteen points of lightness apart on both sides** (25/40/55 light, 96/81/66 dark). Body copy measures ~8.9:1 on the dark paper and headings ~13.7:1. If a fourth ink is ever added, it needs a pair or it needs to live on the cream; there is no third option.
- **A themed token is only safe where the surface under it is themed too.** That is the whole failure mode this decision introduces, and `toolbar.spec.ts` pins it from both ends: the Sheet must repaint, the Aside's panel *and* its ink must not, and the Aside's ink must clear 4.5:1 against its own cream in the dark theme.
